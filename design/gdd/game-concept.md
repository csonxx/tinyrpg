# Game Concept: 雾中誓言

*Created: 2026-04-29*
*Status: Draft*

---

## Elevator Pitch

> It's a narrative RPG set in the anti-Japanese occupation era, where you play a CCP intelligence agent who has spent years in Japan as a留学生 before returning to occupy a position as a人人唾弃的汉奸 — all while secretly serving the underground resistance. Every dialogue choice tests the boundary of trust: gain the enemy's confidence while carrying the weight of being suspected and hunted by your own people.

## Core Fantasy

**You are the person nobody trusts — and you carry it alone.**

The emotional promise is not heroism, but endurance. You experience the psychological burden of maintaining a double identity: hated by the people you serve, trusted by the enemies you undermine. Every conversation is a performance. Every choice carries consequences you won't see until it's too late. The fantasy is the weight of being the silent backbone of history — and the loneliness that comes with it.

---

## Unique Hook

**The Trust Boundary System**: Unlike branching narratives where choices align you with "good" or "evil" factions, 雾中誓言 asks you to simultaneously manage trust from two opposing sides — your enemy handlers AND your own underground organization. Progress with one fractures trust with the other. The core tension is not "who do I help?" but "how long can I serve two masters before both discover the truth?"

Passes the "and also" test: It's like 隐形守护者, AND ALSO the core mechanic is a two-sided trust economy where every victory creates new suspicion.

## Player Experience Analysis (MDA Framework)

### Target Aesthetics (What the player FEELS)

| Aesthetic | Priority | How We Deliver It |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **Sensation** (sensory pleasure) | 3 | Period-accurate audio design, atmospheric music, visual style that evokes 1940s East Asia |
| **Fantasy** (make-believe, role-playing) | 1 | You ARE the deep-cover agent — identity, performance, survival |
| **Narrative** (drama, story arc) | 1 | Multi-chapter story with major dramatic reversals, each choice echoing forward |
| **Challenge** (obstacle course, mastery) | 4 | Decoding dialogue subtext, managing competing trust currencies, finding optimal outcomes |
| **Fellowship** (social connection) | 5 | Relationships with both enemy contacts and underground comrades — all complicated by your position |
| **Discovery** (exploration, secrets) | 2 | Uncovering hidden clues, secret paths, alternative solutions to seemingly impossible situations |
| **Expression** (self-expression, creativity) | 6 | Different playstyles: aggressive spy vs. cautious survivor vs. charismatic manipulator |
| **Submission** (relaxation, comfort zone) | 7 | Low — this game is intentionally tense, not relaxing |

### Key Dynamics (Emergent player behaviors)

- Players will naturally start replaying scenes to find "what if" paths they missed
- Players will discuss with friends which choices were "correct" — validating that the moral ambiguity works
- Players will become emotionally invested in specific characters and feel genuine loss when relationships fracture

### Core Mechanics (Systems we build)

1. **Branching Dialogue System** — every major conversation has 2-4 meaningful branches; choices affect trust, information, and narrative paths
2. **Dual Trust Economy** — separate trust meters for enemy faction and underground organization; both must be managed simultaneously
3. **Relationship Memory** — characters remember past interactions; accumulated trust/distrust affects future availability and story options
4. **Clue & Intel Collection** — investigative segments where players search scenes, interview contacts, and build dossiers
5. **Episode Structure** — each chapter is a self-contained narrative episode with its own dramatic arc, feeding into the larger story

### Player Type Appeal (Bartle Taxonomy)

- [x] **Storytellers** — How: Branching narrative with meaningful choices, multiple endings, emotional arcs
- [x] **Explorers** — How: Discovering all narrative branches, hidden paths, character backstories
- [x] **Achievers** — How: Collecting all endings, completing relationship trees, finding the "true" path
- [ ] **Socializers** — How: Not applicable (single-player); community discussions may fill this gap
- [ ] **Killers/Competitors** — How: Not applicable — this game has no competitive element

### Flow State Design

- **Onboarding curve**: First 10 minutes establish the double identity, the world, and the first meaningful choice — teach through story, not tutorial
- **Difficulty scaling**: Narrative tension naturally escalates across chapters; trust crises become more frequent and more severe
- **Feedback clarity**: Characters visibly react to trust changes; the UI shows the consequences of choices
- **Recovery from failure**: Failure states (exposure, death of key character) are narrative events, not game-overs — they become part of the story

## Core Loop

### Moment-to-Moment (30 seconds)

**Reading and choosing.** The player reads dialogue or narration, analyzes the subtext and relationship context, then selects a response. The satisfaction comes from reading the situation correctly — choosing the response that advances your goals without exposing your identity.

### Short-Term (5-15 minutes)

**A single scene or conversation.** Each scene has a dramatic question (Will you reveal information? Will you protect a comrade? Will you deceive a trusted contact?) and concludes with a meaningful shift in trust, information, or relationship status.

### Session-Level (30-120 minutes)

**One episode / chapter.** Each chapter is a complete narrative unit: setup → rising tension → major trust crisis → resolution (temporary). Natural stopping point after each episode. Players should feel "I need to see what happens next."

### Long-Term Progression

**The full story arc across multiple chapters.** The player grows through story progress, not numerical power. Relationships deepen or fracture. The underground network expands or contracts. The player uncovers layers of conspiracy. The game ends when the protagonist's mission reaches its conclusion — historical victory, tragic defeat, or ambiguous survival.

### Retention Hooks

- **Curiosity**: Each episode ends with a cliffhanger; the conspiracy grows deeper with each chapter
- **Investment**: Characters the player cares about are at risk; losing them feels personal
- **Mastery**: Finding alternative paths, secret dialogue options, and hidden relationships rewards replays
- **Completionism**: Multiple endings, relationship trees, and unlockable scene variants

## Game Pillars

### Pillar 1: 身份即枷锁 (Identity Is a Cage)

The protagonist's double identity is not an advantage — it is an ever-present burden. Every success as a collaborator deepens the public hatred. The game never lets the player "have it both ways."

*Design test*: If a gameplay moment allows the player to be simultaneously celebrated by both the resistance and their enemy collaborators, this pillar says NO.

### Pillar 2: 抉择无完美 (No Choice Is Perfect)

Every meaningful choice carries irreversible consequences. The game does not signal which option is "correct." Players will agonize over decisions because every path has a cost.

*Design test*: If any choice in the game has a clearly superior outcome with no downside, this pillar says revise it.

### Pillar 3: 信任是最脆弱的货币 (Trust Is the Most Fragile Currency)

Trust takes months to build and seconds to destroy. A single misstep can alienate a key contact. Being suspected by your own organization is as dangerous as being discovered by the enemy. The game places psychological pressure at the center.

*Design test*: If the game makes it easy to maintain trust with all parties simultaneously, this pillar says NO.

### Anti-Pillars (What This Game Is NOT)

- **NOT an action game**: There is no real-time combat or reflex-based gameplay. Action sequences, if present, are narrative cinematics.
- **NOT a power fantasy**: The player does not become more powerful over time in a conventional sense. Influence and trust are the only metrics of progress.
- **NOT morally clear**: The game does not punish the player for being "too good" or "too ruthless." Moral ambiguity is structural, not cosmetic.
- **NOT a dating sim**: While relationships are central, the tone is espionage thriller, not romantic.

## Risks and Open Questions

### Design Risks
- Core loop may not sustain engagement across full chapter length — need to validate with MVP playtest
- Moral ambiguity may frustrate players who want clear feedback on "right" choices — need carefully calibrated consequence revelation
- Dual trust system may feel artificial if not organically integrated into story — must avoid making it feel like a UI meter

### Technical Risks
- Branching narrative state explosion: without careful planning, exponential content requirements could make full scope infeasible — mitigate with modular scene architecture
- No game dev experience: first-time developer may underestimate complexity of even "simple" systems — mitigate with aggressive prototyping and MVP focus

### Market Risks
- Historical/sspy theme may limit audience vs. generic fantasy RPG — but 隐形守护者 proves strong appetite exists
- Mobile narrative games have lower monetization ceiling vs. live-service games — but premium model reduces complexity

### Open Questions
- **Q1**: How do we handle the player's Japanese language ability (since the protagonist studied in Japan)? Is dialogue in Japanese, translated, or bilingual?
- **Q2**: Should the game include a "code name" or explicit identity mechanic where the player chooses how the resistance refers to them?
- **Q3**: How do we represent failure states narratively? (Death? Exposure followed by rescue? Being cut off from the organization?)

## MVP Definition

**The absolute minimum version that validates the core hypothesis.**

**Core hypothesis**: Players find the dual-trust, choice-driven espionage narrative engaging for 30+ minute sessions, and the tension of maintaining a double identity creates emotional investment.

**Required for MVP**:
1. **Branching dialogue system** — minimum 3 major choice points with visible trust consequences
2. **Dual trust tracking** — enemy trust meter + underground trust meter, displayed in UI
3. **One complete short chapter** — ~5 scenes, 2 enemy contacts, 1 underground contact, 1 major trust crisis
4. **At least 2 endings** — exposure (failure) and successful deception (partial success)
5. **Mobile-optimized UI** — touch-friendly dialogue selection, save/load

**Explicitly NOT in MVP**:
- Full voice acting
- Character illustration system
- Multiple chapters
- Relationship intimacy system
- Inventory/clue collection UI

### Scope Tiers

| Tier | Content | Features | Timeline |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **MVP** | 1 chapter, ~5 scenes | Branching dialogue, dual trust meters, 2 endings | 3-4 months |
| **Vertical Slice** | 2 chapters, one complete episode arc | + Relationship memory, clue collection, scene background art | 6-8 months |
| **Alpha** | 4-5 chapters | + Multiple endings tracked, character illustration, original score | 12-15 months |
| **Full Vision** | 8-10 chapters, full story arc | + Voice acting, all endings, mobile polish, achievement system | 18-24 months |
