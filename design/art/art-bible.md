## Section 1: Visual Identity Statement

**ONE-LINE RULE:**
**"See the surface, never the whole truth."**

Every visual element should operate in two layers: what is shown, and what is hidden. This is not merely an aesthetic choice — it is the core visual metaphor for double identity, surveillance, and the occupation itself. The player should never feel they have the full picture.

---

**PRINCIPLE 1: The Half-Visible Truth**

*Definition:* Every composition reveals beauty and danger simultaneously — what is shown on the surface is elegant and controlled, but what lies beneath (or just outside the frame) carries the threat.

*Design Test:* When designing a scene and you can choose between a fully lit, comfortable composition and one where something important is just out of frame, partially obscured, or backlit into shadow — choose the second. When an element could be decorative OR threatening, make it both at once.

*Pillar Connection:* Serves **身份即枷锁 (Identity Is a Cage)**. The double identity is not just a narrative condition — it is a visual condition. The player should feel the gap between what a character shows the world and what that character actually is, in every frame.

---

**PRINCIPLE 2: No Frame Should Feel Safe**

*Definition:* Visual compositions should carry unresolved tension — nothing is perfectly centered, perfectly symmetrical, or perfectly at rest. There is always strain at the edges.

*Design Test:* When staging a scene, if all elements are comfortable and balanced, introduce one deliberate imbalance: a character at the edge rather than center, a door slightly ajar suggesting intrusion, a shadow that falls across a character's face. When a composition feels settled, destabilize it.

*Pillar Connection:* Serves **抉择无完美 (No Choice Is Perfect)**. The visual frame itself should feel like a choice already made under pressure. Perfect balance implies stability the game does not have. Tension in composition communicates that the player is always operating in compromised circumstances.

---

**PRINCIPLE 3: Proximity Is Risk**

*Definition:* The space between characters is the most expressive element in the game. Closeness is vulnerability. Distance is safety — or suspicion.

*Design Test:* When illustrating a conversation scene, before placing characters, define the relationship first. Are they allies? Place them closer than they would stand with strangers, but with bodies angled slightly away — trust is present but not complete. Are they adversaries? Use professional distance, but if it narrows, that narrowing is the most dangerous thing in the frame. When in doubt, pull characters apart rather than bunching them together.

*Pillar Connection:* Serves **信任是最脆弱的货币 (Trust Is the Most Fragile Currency)**. Trust is made physical, spatial, legible. The player reads trust the way they read body language in real life — not from what characters say, but from how they hold space around each other.

## Section 2: Mood & Atmosphere

### 1. Normal Dialogue
- **Primary Emotion**: *Controlled unease* — the player should feel competent and in control of their performance, but never comfortable. The body is calm; the mind is calculating.
- **Lighting**: Afternoon or early evening, overcast natural light. Cool-neutral color temperature (~5500K). Low contrast — soft shadows, no harsh edges. Key light from a window at 45 degrees, diffused by paper screens or sheer curtains.
- **Atmosphere**: Measured, watchful, composed, ambiguous, poised
- **Energy Level**: Controlled, deliberate, sustained
- **Key Visual Element**: A character mid-sentence, lit from the side, with their face partially in shadow — you see their expression, but something in the shadow suggests a different truth beneath it.
- **Visual Identity Connection**: The Half-Visible Truth — the scene looks like a normal conversation, but the composition keeps something just out of frame or slightly obscured, reminding the player that every dialogue choice is a performance with an audience.

### 2. High Tension Scene
- **Primary Emotion**: *Exposed vulnerability* — the walls closing in, the wrong word could end everything. Not panic, but the cold, precise awareness of a tight spot.
- **Lighting**: Single harsh source — a bare bulb, a desk lamp with no shade, or interrogation room floodlights. Warm-to-neutral temperature (~3500K) but used clinically. High contrast: deep blacks, sharp white highlights, no midtones. Key light directly overhead or directly facing.
- **Atmosphere**: Suffocating, scrutinizing, accusatory, brittle, electric
- **Energy Level**: Tense, staccato, high-stakes
- **Key Visual Element**: A single spotlight cutting through darkness, illuminating a character or object that should not be seen — or the player's own hand on a table, suddenly too visible.
- **Visual Identity Connection**: No Frame Should Feel Safe — the composition itself is destabilized. The frame may tilt slightly, or the subject may be placed off-center with a hard edge at the frame boundary.

### 3. Quiet Reflection
- **Primary Emotion**: *Bittersweet solitude* — warmth that cannot be shared, grief that has no outlet, love for people you cannot acknowledge.
- **Lighting**: Candlelight or a single desk lamp with warm amber glow (~2700K). Very low contrast — soft, enveloping shadow that does not threaten but wraps. Time of day: deep night, long after the performance has ended.
- **Atmosphere**: Intimate, fragile, aching, tender, guarded
- **Energy Level**: Contemplative, slow, weighted
- **Key Visual Element**: A letter half-read on a worn wooden desk, the ink still wet in places, or a faded photograph with a corner folded over — an object that carries the weight of a double life.
- **Visual Identity Connection**: The Half-Visible Truth — what the player reads or remembers is fragmentary. A letter with sentences torn out. A photograph with faces cropped off. The truth is present but incomplete.

### 4. Underground Contact
- **Primary Emotion**: *Desperate kinship* — relief of being recognized by your own, immediately complicated by the terror that this contact could be compromised.
- **Lighting**: Near-total darkness with a single weak source — a shielded flashlight, a candle under a cupped hand, a lamp with a cloth over it. Cool color temperature (~4000K). Contrast moderate: figures emerge from shadow but are not fully revealed.
- **Atmosphere**: Clandestine, urgent, fragile, conspiratorial, close
- **Energy Level**: Intimate, hushed, charged
- **Key Visual Element**: Two figures close enough that their breath might meet, speaking in voices barely above a whisper, with a table or wall between them.
- **Visual Identity Connection**: Proximity Is Risk — in enemy scenes, characters maintain professional distance. Here, the distance collapses. But that closeness itself becomes a kind of danger — the warmth of reunion and the exposure of being truly seen.

### 5. Enemy Event
- **Primary Emotion**: *Performative composure* — the exhausting vigilance of smiling at people you are working to destroy. Every gesture is calculated.
- **Lighting**: Formal evening function — chandeliers, warm golden overhead lights (~3000K), reflected glow from polished surfaces. High key overall but with pockets of shadow. Contrast relatively flat and flattering — the kind of light designed to make people look trustworthy.
- **Atmosphere**: Polished, suffocating, performative, glittering, hollow
- **Energy Level**: Measured but brittle — smooth surfaces under which everything is tense
- **Key Visual Element**: A champagne glass catching light, or a Japanese military uniform reflected in a window — beauty and threat in the same object.
- **Visual Identity Connection**: The Half-Visible Truth — the scene looks glamorous. But the composition always shows something beneath the surface: a shadow falling across a character's face at the wrong moment, a door that should not be there.

### 6. Chapter Cliffhanger
- **Primary Emotion**: *Dread suspended* — the moment before the fall. Not the fall itself, but the held breath. The player is left at the edge of understanding something terrible.
- **Lighting**: No fixed time — determined by what the cliffhanger reveals. Could be the gray pre-dawn of a morning after something has already gone wrong, or the amber of a room where a phone has just rung and not been answered. Color temperature varies by chapter beat. Contrast high — the light is not helping, it is exposing.
- **Atmosphere**: Unresolved, suspended, ominous, pivotal, incomplete
- **Energy Level**: Arrested — everything has stopped; the world is holding its breath
- **Key Visual Element**: A character's face at the exact moment of realization — eyes widening, a hand on a doorknob, a document with a name the player recognizes. The background painted as if time has stopped.
- **Visual Identity Connection**: No Frame Should Feel Safe — the cliffhanger frame is structurally incomplete. A figure walking toward a door that is not fully shown. The composition leans toward something outside the frame.

## Section 3: Shape Language

**Overarching Principle:** Shape is meaning. In a game where characters perform identities and hide truths, the geometry of every element — a shoulder angle, a window frame, a dialogue box border — must carry double meaning.

### 3.1 Character Silhouette Philosophy

**Core Rule: The Body Is a Statement, Not a Description**

Characters are rendered large (minimum 1/4 of frame height in dialogue scenes). Silhouette distinction is learning-based — the player accumulates understanding over time.

| Archetype | Shoulder Width | Head-to-Body Ratio | Stance | Key Distinguishing Trait |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Protagonist (Surface)** | Narrow-to-medium; contained | 1:3.5 | Weight balanced, parallel feet | Shoulders angled 5-8 degrees forward — performing self-effacement |
| **Protagonist (Hidden)** | Same skeleton; angle shifts 12-15 degrees backward | Same | Same feet; torso upright | Head carriage taller by half a head-height; spine neutral |
| **Enemy Officer** | Wide; squared block | 1:4 | Wide stance, low center of gravity | Sharp right-angle breaks at elbow and knee joints |
| **Underground Contact** | Narrow; rounded | 1:3 | Asymmetric weight distribution | One shoulder always slightly raised — permanent quiet alert |
| **Civilian (Bystander)** | Variable; soft | 1:3 to 1:3.5 | Uncoordinated, relaxed | No geometric breaks — curves only; no sharp angles |

**Double Identity (Subliminal):** The protagonist's two personas share the same skeleton. Surface: shoulders forward 5-8°, head forward of spine, weight on heels (apologetic). Hidden: shoulders 12-15° behind neutral, head over spine, weight centered (owned). Player learns the 2-3° difference over time.

### 3.2 Environment Geometry

**Dominant: Angular (60-70%)**
- Building facades: Sharp rectangles, flat roofs, grid windows with thin mullions
- Street geometry: Narrow alleys with 90-degree wall intersections — forced entry on architect's terms
- Furniture: Square tables, rectangular desks, right-angle chair backs

**Organic (20-25%)**
- Traditional East Asian elements: Slight upward beam curvature, arched paper screens, wooden grain
- Nature intruding: Tree branch through window, organic crack edges — deliberate, rare

**Geometric/Mechanical (10-15%) — Enemy Objects Only**
- Military equipment, desk lamps, typewriters, surveillance cameras — precise circles, semicircles, grids

**Safe vs. Enemy Space:**

| Element | Underground Safe | Enemy Territory |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling | Low — forced crouch/intimate | High — institutional |
| Light source | Below eye level (lamp, candle) | Above eye level (ceiling fixture) |
| Corner treatment | Rounded or hidden | Visible, well-lit |
| Wall condition | Organic, irregular stone | Perfectly plumb, manufactured |

### 3.3 UI Shape Grammar

**Dialogue Box System:**

| Type | Shape Origin | Width | Border |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Player Voice** | Rectangle, top-left origin | 85% screen | 2px top/left only; open right/bottom |
| **NPC Voice** | Rectangle, top-right origin | 70% screen | 2px top/right only; open left |
| **Narration** | Full-width, no border | 92% screen | No border; italicized text |

**Trust Meter UI:**
- **Imperial Loyalty (top):** Bar with sharp 90° right termination — finality, authority
- **Underground Trust (bottom):** Bar with 15° tapered point — openness, invitation
- **Balanced state:** Both at same fill level, different terminations visible
- **Danger state:** Affected bar pulses (scale 1.0→1.02, 2-second diagonal shear)

**Menu System:**
- Container: 3px rounded corners maximum
- Active item: Parallelogram shift (top-right extends 4px forward — chevron)
- Back navigation: Left-pointing equilateral triangle, no animation
- Transitions: 200ms ease-in-out, no bounce

### 3.4 Hero Shapes vs. Supporting Shapes

| Scene Type | Hero Shape | Supporting |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue | Speaking character's face/torso | Listening face, hands, background |
| Quiet Reflection | Reflection object (letter, photo) | Protagonist's hand, face in shadow |
| High Tension | Source of threat (spotlight, interrogator) | Everything else recedes |
| Underground Contact | Gap between two characters | Both figures as unified shape |
| Enemy Event | Uniform or held glass | Ambient guests, environment |
| Chapter Cliffhanger | Face mid-realization | Freeze-frame environment |

**Rules:** Hero always 40-60% larger than supporting. Supporting uses 1-2 detail lines; Hero uses 3-5. Hero is static or controlled motion; supporting may be blurred/obscured.

### 5.1 Emotional Shape Vocabulary

| Emotion | Dominant Shape | Key Geometry |
|---|---|---|
| **Tension** | Diagonal/descending | 30-60° angles, asymmetric shoulders, content touching 2+ frame edges |
| **Intimacy** | Close/overlapping/warm curves | <1 head-width distance, 15-25% overlap, 8-12px rounded corners |
| **Danger** | Sharp/pointed/descending | Triangle <30° pointing at subject, hard shadow edges, descending diagonals >60° |
| **Safety** | Enclosed/stable/horizontal | Bounding low walls, unbroken horizontals, seated posture, warm low light |

**Mood State Shape Overlays:**

| Mood State | Dominant Shape | Secondary | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal Dialogue | Horizontal rectangles | Diagonal shoulders | 5-15° |
| High Tension | Sharp diagonals descending | Pointed elements | 55-70° |
| Quiet Reflection | Curves, soft rectangles | Horizontal lines | 0-10° |
| Underground Contact | Overlapping ovals | Organic edges | 0-5° |
| Enemy Event | Perfect squares/rectangles | Hard horizontals | 0° |
| Cliffhanger | Broken, interrupted | Pointed at edge | Variable |

## Section 4: Color System

**Core Principle:** Desaturation as primary tool — fog is absence, not a color. Every color is muted. Saturated colors feel dangerous precisely because they are rare.

### 4.1 Primary Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Semantic Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface/False Safety | **Bone White** | `#E8E0D5` | Paper documents, civilian face, the performed self. Fragile. |
| Authority/Death/Mystery | **Deep Ink** | `#1C1B22` | Blue-undertone near-black. Occupation forces, death, night, the unknown self. |
| Fog/Uncertainty/Surveillance | **Grey-Lavender** | `#8B8A94` | Fog of incomplete information. Surveillance. The space between what you know and what's real. |
| Blood/Defiance/Authority | **Dusty Rose** | `#9B6B6B` | Desaturated red — rare so each appearance is overloaded: blood, defiance, authority ALL activate simultaneously. |
| Warmth/Connection/Risk | **Amber Ochre** | `#B8925A` | Genuine warmth. Shadowed by cost — amber NEVER escapes cleanly. |
| Trust/Underground/Ideal | **Muted Jade** | `#5E8B7E` | Underground trust, chosen cause. Cooler than amber, abstract ideal vs. human warmth. |
| Cold Truth/Night/Clarity | **Slate Blue** | `#4A6175` | Night, information, surveillance. Truth that is dangerous to know. |

**Saturation Map:** Dusty Rose (rarest) → Bone White → Grey-Lavender → Deep Ink (void)

### 4.2 Semantic Color Rules

- **Red (#9B6B6B)**: Never appears without activating ALL THREE meanings (blood, defiance, authority). Rare — 4-6 times per chapter. When red appears: stop. Something structural has shifted.
- **Amber (#B8925A)**: ALWAYS carries shadow of corruption cost. Warmth is real, but the double life required to maintain it is always present.
- **Jade (#5E8B7E)**: Represents the IDEAL of the resistance, not individual contacts (those are amber — human and warm).
- **White (#E8E0D5)**: The most dangerous color — documents, civilian cover, the absence of self. Fragile.
- **Black (#1C1B22)**: The void that watches. Unknown identities, consequences, future.
- **Blue (#4A6175)**: Cold truth. Information revealed is dangerous information.
- **Green**: NOT USED. No innocent green in 1940s occupied East Asia.

### 4.3 Per-Game-State Color Rules

| State | Dominant Temperature | Key Accent | Emotional Palette |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal Dialogue** | Neutral-warm (bone white) | Slate blue speaker indicators, occasional amber | Controlled unease. Surface is calm. Desaturated. |
| **High Tension** | Neutral-cool → grey-lavender | Dusty rose (danger proximity) | Dread suspended. Edges darkening. Desaturation increasing. |
| **Quiet Reflection** | Grey-lavender wash | Muted jade (the cause), deep ink (buried self) | Stillness with undercurrent anxiety. Near-monochromatic. |
| **Underground Contact** | Warm-cool split (amber+jade) | Amber warmth, jade cause | Proximity is risk. Warmest state, but amber's shadow present. |
| **Enemy Event** | Cool (slate blue, deep ink) | Dusty rose (exposure risk), slate blue | Active threat. Surface collapsed. Near-monochrome dark. |
| **Chapter Cliffhanger** | Near-neutral (grey-lavender) | Dusty rose single accent from monochrome | Sustained irresolution. Palette offers no comfort. |

### 4.4 UI Palette

UI uses the same palette as world art — no safe space exists outside the fog.

| UI Element | Color | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue Box Background | `#E8E0D5` | The surface where the performed self speaks |
| Speaker Name | `#4A6175` | Cold truth of identity |
| Dialogue Text | `#1C1B22` | Authority of the word |
| Choice Buttons — Default | `#8B8A94` | Uncertainty of choice |
| Choice Buttons — Hover | `#B8925A` | Warmth — this choice has human stakes |
| Choice Buttons — Selected | `#5E8B7E` | Underground trust — commitment |
| System Menu Background | `#1C1B22` | Authority — the rules are the rules |
| Notification/Warning | `#9B6B6B` | Structural moment — rare |

### 4.5 Colorblind Safety

| Color | Meaning | Backup Required | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dusty Rose | Blood/defiance/authority | YES — diamond burst shape + musical sting | HIGH |
| Amber | Warmth/risk | YES — warmer brushstroke texture | MEDIUM |
| Jade | Underground trust | YES — circular badge + "UNDERGROUND" label | HIGH |
| Slate Blue | Cold truth | YES — moon icon + text label | MEDIUM |
| Grey-Lavender | Fog/uncertainty | No backup (atmosphere only) | N/A |

### 4.6 Trust Meter Colors

**Design: Parallel Muted Colors** — neither allegiance is "safe." Both desaturate toward grey when in danger.

| Meter | Color | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Imperial Loyalty** | `#C4A882` (Dusty Ochre) | Angular end caps (hexagonal) |
| **Underground Trust** | `#5E8B7E` (Muted Jade) | Rounded end caps (circular) |

**Danger state:** Both desaturate. Critical state: Affected meter pulses with base color; other dims.

## Section 5: Character Design Direction

### 5.1 Player Character: Double Identity

**Surface Persona (Collaborator):** Shoulders 5-8° forward, head forward of spine, weight on heels, downward gaze. Performing self-effacement.

**Hidden Persona (Resistance Agent):** Shoulders 12-15° behind neutral, head over spine, weight centered, direct gaze. Dignity reclaimed.

**Transition:** Posture shift visible within single drawing. Head-to-spine relationship is most legible indicator at small sizes. Shoulder-spine-head difference must remain legible at ALL LOD levels.

### 5.2 Key Character Archetypes

| Archetype | Silhouette Key | Color | Expression Style | Instant Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Enemy Officer** | Wide squared shoulders, low center of gravity, sharp 90° joint breaks | Slate blue uniform, Dusty Rose accent only | Horizontal brow, minimal eyebrow, face as mask | Shoulder pads + perfect posture = authority |
| **Underground Contact** | Narrow, rounded, one shoulder raised (permanent alert) | Muted Jade, Amber in warm moments | Expressive eyebrows, eyes carry full weight | Narrow shoulders + warm color + raised alert |
| **Civilian Bystander** | Variable body types, no geometric breaks, curves only | Bone White, no faction colors | Expressive but generic | No angular geometry + no faction color = civilian |

### 5.3 Expression Style: Controlled Minimalism

- Emotion lives ABOVE the nose (eyebrows + eyes), not mouth
- Mouth used for dialogue delivery only
- Prohibited: open-mouthed shock, wide smiles showing teeth, furrowed brow without eye contact

| Expression | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Neutral** | Flat brows, open eyes with visible iris, neutral jaw |
| **Tension** | Brow lowers 2-3mm, eyes narrow, one mouth corner tightens |
| **Concern** | Brows raise 2-3mm, eyes widen slightly, head tilts 3-5° |
| **Recognition** | Eyes widen — primary contact-discovery indicator |
| **Fear** | Brows raised and curved, eyes wide, pallor desaturates 10% |
| **Controlled Anger** | Jaw set, brow lowered, mouth neutral — rage sublimated into will |

### 5.4 LOD Philosophy (Mobile ~400px Wide)

**Preserved at ALL levels:**
- Silhouette key shape (shoulder width, stance)
- Eye shape + eyebrow position
- Shoulder angle difference between protagonist personas
- Head-to-spine relationship

**Simplifies first (in order):**
1. Background → solid tone
2. Hair detail → head outline + 2-3 direction strokes
3. Fabric texture → flat color + single hard shadow
4. Hand detail → mitten simplification
5. Face detail → eye, eyebrow, single nose shadow

**LOD Tiers:**
- Full (promo art): 2048px, full brushstroke texture
- Dialogue (~400px wide): Primary game view. Face fills 60%+ of frame.
- Selection icons (~80px): Silhouette only, single flat color

### 5.5 Period Elements

**Military (Japanese Imperial):** Peak cap, collar rank insignia, shoulder boards. Simplified to silhouette: wide squared shoulders, peaked brim, angular collar.

**Civilian Men's:** High collar (changshan) or open collar (tunic). Narrow shoulder + straight drape silhouette.

**Civilian Women's:** Qipao with high collar, side slit. Curved shoulder line.

**Must be accurate:** Collar height/style (era marker), headwear, boot height vs cloth shoes.

**Anchors (at least 2 per interior):** Bakelite telephone, wooden radio cabinet, enamelware, typewriter, wartime newspaper, bicycle.

### 5.6 Production Notes for Illustrators

- Line: 1-2px primary forms, 0.5px secondary, Deep Ink (#1C1B22) — NOT pure black
- Color: Flat + 1-2 hard-edge shadows + 15-20% grain overlay (multiply)
- Atmospheric wash: Grey-Lavender (#8B8A94) at 5-10% over entire illustration
- No white outlines, no cel-shading, no phototexture
- Protagonist: 2 illustrations per scene state (Surface + Hidden), 6-expression minimum sheet per persona
- Enemy Officer: 4-expression minimum, uniform detail slightly higher fidelity
- Underground Contact: 6-expression minimum, hands with care (warmth communication)
- Civilian: 3-5 variants, single scene-specific emotional state each

## Section 6: Environment Design Language

### 6.1 Architectural Style: Two Systems in Tension

**System A — Japanese Military Administrative (Bauhaus-adjacent):** Flat roofs, grid windows (4-over-4 or 6-over-6 thin muntins), horizontal lintel bands, reinforced concrete. No ornament, no shadows to hide in. Designed for surveillance.

**System B — Traditional East Asian Vernacular:** Pitched/curved roofs, exposed beam ends, shoji screens (paper over wood lattice), worn stone thresholds, lanterns at below-eye-level. Human-scaled, organic, pre-occupation.

**Rule:** These two systems are NEVER blended. They exist in direct adjacency — colonial flat facade beside traditional curved eaves. The collision IS the story.

### 6.2 Texture Philosophy: Painted Brushstroke

- Hero surfaces: Visible brushstroke direction (horizontal for walls, vertical for pillars)
- One texture imperfection per hero object (crack, wood grain knot, paint scratch)
- Enemy territory: Minimized brushstroke texture — occupation brings uniformity
- Safe/underground: Maximum texture (wood grain, stone wear, fabric weave, paper fiber)
- Fog effect: Color desaturation + value flattening (wash toward Grey-Lavender) — NOT volumetric particles
- NO PBR, NO normal maps, NO bump mapping

### 6.3 Prop Density = Information

| Space Type | Density | Object Type | Message |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Enemy Territory** | Sparse (1-3 props/2m²) | Utilitarian, standardized, official | Control — unnecessary objects removed |
| **Underground Safe** | Cluttered (8-15 props/2m²) | Personal, handmade, hidden | Authentic life persisting |
| **Civilian Space** | Moderate (4-7 props/2m²) | Lived-in, practical, worn | Survival compromise |
| **Enemy Event (Formal)** | Moderate-sparse (3-5 props) | Luxury, display, performance | Hollow wealth |

**Underground 7/3 Truth Rule:** 70% cover (decoy, mundane items), 30% signal (resistance signals — chalk marks, specific incense, calendar showing old date).

### 6.4 Environmental Storytelling

Every scene requires minimum 3 details from these categories:

**Category 1 — Evidence of Occupation:** Japanese signage, propaganda posters, military supply crates, curfew notices. Proves this is occupied territory.

**Category 2 — Pre-War Life:** Pre-1937 photographs, traditional craft objects, old newspapers. Proves the city existed before.

**Category 3 — Hidden Resistance (Underground ONLY):** Chalk threshold marks, window positioned to see without being seen, irregular floorboard (meeting place code), specific incense scent as entry signal.

**Forbidden:** Obvious secret passages, obvious resistance flags/weapon caches, dead family photographs as heavy-handed backstory.

### 6.5 Interior Types

| Type | Ceiling | Light Source | Key Characteristic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Collaborator's Office** | Medium | Desk lamp (warm) + overhead fluorescent (cold) — split personality | Western furniture, documents in Japanese, personal photo back-to-viewer |
| **Underground Hideout** | Very low | Single shielded candle/lamp at table height | Stone/plaster walls, mismatched furniture, Category 2+3 props |
| **Enemy Administrative** | High institutional | Linear fluorescent, no warm option | Standardized military-issue, regulation white/grey walls, zero texture |
| **Traditional Tea House** | Medium-low with beams | Paper screens diffusing daylight | Shoji screens, tokonoma alcove, worn tatami at thresholds |

### 6.6 Exterior Types

| Type | Key Characteristic | Emotional Texture |
|---|---|---|
| **Colonial Thoroughfare** | Continuous building line, Japanese+local signage, military figures | Most public performance required |
| **Alley (Between Buildings)** | Narrow (single file), rear faces of buildings, variable light shafts | Transitional — neither controlled nor safe |
| **Formal Garden (Appropriated)** | Traditional garden, impeccably maintained, Japanese inscriptions now | Most unsettling exterior — beauty entirely under occupation control |

### 6.7 Period Authenticity

**1940s Specificity Anchors (minimum 2 per interior):** Bakelite telephone (heavy handset, no dial or operator dial), wooden radio cabinet (Art Deco/wartime styling), enamelware cups/bowls (wartime metal scarcity), typewriter, wartime newspaper with date visible, bicycle with cargo basket.

**What makes 1940s NOT 1930s/1950s:** Gas shortages (bicycles dominant over cars), visible war damage and repair patches, mandatory Japanese signage in some areas, rationing, radio as primary media.

**Scarcity as Visual System:** Patched clothing, repair patches on buildings, inconsistent infrastructure, simple food presentation. Enemy/collaborator spaces show LEAST scarcity. Underground shows MORE.

## Section 7: UI/HUD Visual Direction

**Core Principle:** The UI is the player's constant companion through the fog. It must communicate reliably at a glance (mobile, one hand, poor lighting) while never breaking the illusion that the player is handling documents and objects in the world. No plastic HUD — everything feels like it belongs to the 1940s.

---

### 7.1 Diegetic vs Screen-Space HUD

**Decision: Screen-Space with World-Matched Aesthetic**

The spy theme requires the HUD to be always legible and always present — trust meters must be visible during dialogue without requiring the player to search for them. Diegetic placement (floating in scene, attached to character models) would create readability failures on small mobile screens and obscure the hand-painted character art that carries emotional weight.

**Rationale:**
- Trust meters (Imperial Loyalty + Underground Trust) must be simultaneously visible during all dialogue — this is core game state, not environmental flavor
- Diegetic UI would require camera repositioning that conflicts with the fixed dialogue camera language (Section 3.3)
- Screen-space allows pixel-perfect touch targets essential for mobile
- "World-matched aesthetic" means: same color palette, same brushstroke texture philosophy, same typography family, same shape grammar

**Layer Architecture:**

| Layer | Content | Render Method |
|---|---|---|
| Scene | Character art, environments, dialogue portraits | World art pipeline |
| Dialogue Box | Speaker name, dialogue text, choice buttons | Screen-space, document aesthetic |
| HUD Overlay | Trust meters, chapter indicator, inventory quick-access | Screen-space, minimal chrome |
| System Menu | Pause, settings, save/load | Screen-space, deep ink panel |

**Diegetic Analogue — The Document Metaphor:**
Dialogue boxes are designed to feel like paper documents held at the bottom of the frame. The speaker name plate resembles a typed label or stamp. Choice buttons feel like annotation marks or checkboxes on a form. This preserves immersion without sacrificing legibility.

---

### 7.2 Typography Direction

**Font Family:** Noto Serif SC

| Weight | Usage | Size Hierarchy |
|---|---|---|
| Medium (500) | Speaker name plate, choice button labels | 18-20sp (largest) |
| Regular (400) | Dialogue body text, narration | 16sp |
| Light (300) | System menu labels, secondary UI | 14sp |

**Fallback Stack:** `Noto Serif SC → Source Han Serif CN → STSong → serif`

**Rationale:** Noto Serif SC is a transitional serif with calligraphic influence — it carries the period-appropriate weight of 1940s document typography while remaining highly legible at mobile sizes (16sp minimum). The medium weight provides clear hierarchy between speaker name and dialogue text without requiring font changes within the same text run.

**Size Rules:**
- Dialogue text: minimum 16sp (touch target text must be readable without magnification)
- Speaker name: 18-20sp, Medium weight, Slate Blue (#4A6175)
- Narration: 16sp, Regular weight, italicized, grey-lavender (#8B8A94)
- Choice buttons: 18sp, Medium weight
- System menu: 14sp Light weight for labels, 16sp Regular for values

**Line Height:** 1.6x for dialogue (breathing room for Chinese text legibility), 1.4x for compact UI elements.

**Touch-Readable Minimum:** No text element smaller than 14sp. Interactive elements (buttons, choices) use 18sp minimum to ensure one-thumb readability on 320px-wide screens.

### 7.6 Animation Feel

**Core Timing Principle:** UI animation should feel like paper sliding on a surface — present but not playful. No bounce, no spring overshoot. Smooth, deliberate, 1940s mechanical precision.

**Standard UI Transition: 200ms ease-in-out**

| Transition | Duration | Easing | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Menu open (pause/settings) | 200ms | ease-in-out | Fade in (0→100% opacity) + scale (0.95→1.0) |
| Menu close | 200ms | ease-in-out | Fade out + scale (1.0→0.95), reverse of open |
| Dialogue box advance | 200ms | ease-in-out | Slide out left (0→-10% x) + slide in right from +10% |
| Choice button hover | 150ms | ease-in | Color fill transition (grey→amber) |
| Choice button select | 150ms | ease-out | Scale pulse (1.0→1.02→1.0) + color to jade |
| Trust meter fill change | 300ms | ease-in-out | Width transition with subtle overshoot prevention |
| Trust meter danger pulse | 150ms | ease-in-out | Scale (1.0→1.02→1.0), diagonal 2-second shear pattern |
| Overlay panel (text log) | 250ms | ease-in-out | Slide up from bottom |
| Scene transition (chapter) | 400ms | ease-in-out | Fade to grey-lavender wash, hold 100ms, fade in |

**Trust Meter Pulse (Special Case):**
- Duration: 150ms (faster than standard — feels alive, not distracting)
- Motion: scale 1.0 → 1.02 → 1.0, combined with subtle diagonal shear (2px right, 2px down)
- Triggered by: trust level entering danger zone (below 25%)
- Sound pairing: optional subtle heartbeat SFX (producer decision, not art requirement)

**NO BOUNCE anywhere in UI.** The spy theme demands restraint. If an animation feels playful or whimsical, it is wrong.

**Accessibility Note:** All animations respect system "Reduce Motion" setting. When enabled: instant cuts (0ms) replace all transitions. Trust meter pulses are disabled; instead, affected meter border flashes (3 pulses, 200ms each, 50% duty cycle).

### UX Alignment Notes

**Mobile Readability:**
- Minimum 16sp for all dialogue text (confirmed: Noto Serif SC renders well at 16sp on iOS and Android at default scaling)
- Minimum 44x44px touch targets for all interactive elements
- Trust meters positioned in top 10% of screen — thumb-zone adjacent but not obscured
- No UI element closer than 8px to screen edges (safe area respect)

**One-Handed Operation:**
- All common actions (advance dialogue, select choice) accessible in bottom 60% of screen
- Menu access (hamburger) in top-right or accessible via swipe-down from top (platform-native)
- No horizontal swipe required for core gameplay loop
- Back navigation via hardware back button (Android) or on-screen triangle (iOS)

**Outdoor/Low-Light Legibility:**
- All text on Bone White or Deep Ink backgrounds — maximum contrast ratio (>7:1)
- No text on Grey-Lavender backgrounds at sizes below 18sp
- Trust meters use color + shape (angular vs rounded terminations) as redundant coding — colorblind-safe beyond color alone
- Dusty Rose (danger) always appears with diamond burst shape + text label, never color alone

**Cognitive Load:**
- Maximum 3 choices displayed at once (game design constraint that supports UI simplicity)
- No nested menus deeper than 2 levels
- Consistent back navigation always available
- Chapter progress indicator always visible (subtle, top of screen)

**Performance:**
- No particle effects in UI (trust meter pulse uses transform scale only)
- All UI elements are flat textures or vector — no runtime blur or glow effects
- Menu transitions use opacity + transform only (GPU-composited, no repaints)

## Section 9: Reference Direction

**Purpose:** This section provides visual reference anchors that validate the art direction and give illustrators concrete direction. Each reference adds something the others do not. Together they define a space specific to this game — not "like X but set in 1940s China." The avoidances are as important as the draws. They prevent surface-level imitation and protect the integrity of the visual identity.

---

### Reference 1: Florence (game, 2019)

**Name:** Florence — a handheld-style narrative game by Mountains, published by Annapurna Interactive

**What to DRAW FROM:**
- **Emotional compression technique:** A single close-up panel communicates a full emotional state — an eye glistening, a shoulder slumping, a hand withdrawing. Use the same economy: one expression shift per scene state, never more.
- **Warm flat color palette:** Desaturated peach, rose, and amber tones applied as flat fields with single-shadow depth. Replicate the ratio: approximately 70% warm neutral (bone/sand) with 20-30% warm accent (rose/terracotta).
- **Hand-illustrated brushstroke texture:** Visible directional strokes in background elements at 15-20% opacity multiply layer. Stroke direction follows surface logic — horizontal on walls, vertical on furniture.
- **Compact narrative staging:** Each scene uses two to three visual elements maximum. A conversation is a face and a hand. A memory is a single object. Restraint is the technique.

**What to AVOID:**
- Florence's palette is optimistic and warm throughout. Do not replicate its bright, uplifting color temperature — our warmth is always shadowed and compromised.
- Florence's illustrative style uses soft, rounded linework at 2-3px weight. Our linework is 1-2px primary with 0.5px secondary, in Deep Ink (not pure black).
- Florence's emotional register is bittersweet-with-hope. Our emotional register is beauty-with-dread. The warmth should never read as comfort.
- Do not use Florence's wide landscape compositions. Our game is portrait-primary; Florence's horizontal framing is incompatible with our UI.

**Pillar Connection:** Serves **信任是最脆弱的货币 (Trust Is the Most Fragile Currency)** and **身份即枷锁 (Identity Is a Cage)**. Florence demonstrates that emotional truth is communicated through restraint and proximity, not detail. Our characters perform warmth they cannot feel; Florence's intimacy technique gives us the visual language to show the gap between performed closeness and genuine connection — the same gap our pillars describe.

### Reference 2: 隐形守护者 (The Invisible Guardian / The Last Secret, game, 2019)

**Name:** 隐形守护者 — interactive visual novel by Invalidus Studio / Goodsoft Technology

**What to DRAW FROM:**
- **Period spy setting authenticity:** The game demonstrates that 1940s occupied East Asian settings can sustain a full visual novel experience. It proves the audience exists and the format works. Learn from its set-dressing discipline: every interior tells the political reality of who controls the space.
- **Visual novel format validation:** Dialogue-driven narrative with branching choices, illustrated scenes, and multiple ending states. Demonstrates that Chinese-audience visual novels handle moral compromise, surveillance, and double identity as core mechanics — not edge cases.
- **Dual-identity visual grammar:** The protagonist's public and private personas are distinguished through costume, posture, and environmental context. Adopt this principle: the surface persona is defined by what it displays to others; the hidden persona is defined by what it cannot say.
- **Material culture of occupation:** Japanese military administrative spaces rendered with bureaucratic sterility. Traditional Chinese interiors rendered with warmth-under-pressure. The collision of these two systems validates Section 6's two-architecture principle.

**What to AVOID:**
- 隐形守护者 uses photographic reference as the basis for its art — real locations, real objects. We use hand-illustrated flat art, not photographic adaptation. The visual approach is painterly, not documentary-referential.
- Its character art uses a brighter, more saturated palette than our desaturated approach. Do not match its saturation levels.
- 隐形守护er's tone includes more direct action sequences than our psychological-intimacy focus. The spy setting is a vehicle for surveillance tension and moral compromise, not action.

**Pillar Connection:** Primarily serves **身份即枷锁 (Identity Is a Cage)**. 隐形守护者 demonstrates that the player's investment in a double identity depends on seeing the cage from both inside and outside — the performance and the truth. It validates that Chinese audiences will engage with this premise at visual-novel length. It confirms that period accuracy and emotional intimacy are compatible, not competing goals.

### Reference 3: East Asian Ink Wash (水墨 / sumi-e)

**Name:** East Asian ink wash painting — specifically Song Dynasty landscape tradition (Ma Yuan, Xia Gui) and Muromachi period Japanese suiboku-ga

**What to DRAW FROM:**
- **Fog and absence rendering:** In ink wash painting, the mountain that matters is the one partially obscured by mist. The visible edges of fog define form more powerfully than full illustration. Apply this to our atmospheric wash: fog is absence, not presence. The Grey-Lavender wash is most powerful where it removes detail, not where it adds color.
- **Brushstroke texture as meaning:** In ink wash, a single confident stroke can define a pine needle cluster or a mountain ridge. The stroke is the form. Our linework uses 1-2px strokes with directional intent — stroke direction communicates material (wood grain horizontal, stone texture varied, fabric drape vertical).
- **Composition through emptiness (留白, liubai):** The empty area of the scroll is not neutral — it is charged. It is the space the viewer's mind must complete. Apply this to our character framing: never fill the frame. Leave 15-25% of the composition as intentional emptiness that the player's mind must resolve.
- **Layered atmospheric depth:** Ink wash builds depth through successive washes, not linework. Distant mountains are lighter and less detailed than near forms. Apply this to our scene backgrounds: depth is communicated through desaturation and simplification, not through atmospheric perspective fog particles.

**What to AVOID:**
- Traditional ink wash uses a monochrome palette (black ink variations on white or off-white paper). Do not apply monochrome — we have a color palette. Ink wash informs composition and texture, not our color system.
- Do not use decorative or calligraphic brushwork. Every stroke must have environmental logic (surface type, material, light).
- Avoid "sumi-e style" as an aesthetic trend — the simplified, tourist-facing version of ink wash. Our brushstroke texture must serve the fog-of-war visual identity, not evoke a style category.

**Pillar Connection:** Serves all three pillars, primarily **抉择无完美 (No Choice Is Perfect)** and **信任是最脆弱的货币 (Trust Is the Most Fragile Currency)**. The liubai principle — that which is absent is as meaningful as that which is present — is the visual equivalent of "no choice is fully knowable." The player never sees the complete picture. Ink wash composition makes this absence feel intentional and beautiful, not like a technical limitation.

### Reference 4: The Wolf Among Us (Telltale Games, 2013)

**Name:** The Wolf Among Us — Telltale Games, based on Neil Gaiman's "Fables" comics

**What to DRAW FROM:**
- **Noir atmosphere without action dependency:** The Wolf Among Us demonstrates that moral ambiguity and atmospheric dread can sustain an entire narrative without action set pieces. Tension comes from what characters know about each other, not from what they do to each other. Our game shares this principle — the spy setting is about information asymmetry, not espionage action.
- **Environmental detail density:** Every prop in a Wolf Among Us scene feels curated and meaningful. An ashtray, a liquor bottle, a half-open drawer — these objects tell a story without text. Apply this discipline to our prop placement: every object visible in a scene must either establish the space's political ownership or the character's emotional state. Nothing is decorative filler.
- **Muted palette with selective warm accent:** The Wolf Among Us uses a predominantly desaturated blue-grey environment with selective warm accents (amber lamplight, red neon) that feel dangerous precisely because they are rare. Our palette uses the same logic — Grey-Lavender dominates, Dusty Rose and Amber appear rarely and carry danger when they do.
- **Moral ambiguity in character rendering:** No character in The Wolf Among Us is simply good or evil. Their visual design reflects this — a character who helps you may have a cruel face; a character who threatens you may have kind eyes. Apply this to our character archetypes: the enemy officer should have a composed, almost handsome face; the underground contact should show strain and fatigue.

**What to AVOID:**
- The Wolf Among Us is set in a stylized fantasy urban world with Art Deco influences and exaggerated caricature-based character design. Our setting is 1940s occupied East Asia with illustrative realism — not the same visual register.
- Its lighting uses high-contrast noir spotlights that are plot-driven (a specific light source in the scene). Our lighting is atmospheric and diffused, consistent with the fog-of-war identity.
- Do not replicate The Wolf Among Us's dialogue box design or UI chrome — these are Telltale-specific conventions, not genre conventions.

**Pillar Connection:** Primarily serves **身份即枷锁 (Identity Is a Cage)** and **抉择无完美 (No Choice Is Perfect)**. The Wolf Among Us demonstrates that the player can be morally compromised and still emotionally invested — the visual design supports this by never letting the player feel fully righteous or fully villainous. Each scene's lighting and composition should make the player question which character they would rather be trapped in a room with.

### Reference 5: 1940s East Asian Photography

**Name:** Documentary photography from occupied Shanghai, Beijing, and other East Asian cities, 1937-1945. Key archives: John F. Lee (Shanghai), Frederick A. G. B. Black (Indochina), Magnum Photos East Asia collection, Japanese war correspondence photography (for material culture accuracy only).

**What to DRAW FROM:**
- **Period material culture accuracy:** The specific weight of fabric on shoulders (qipao vs. changshan vs. Western suits), the height of shoe heels vs. cloth shoes, the exact collar styles of the era, the shape of bicycles and rickshaws. These are not aesthetic choices — they are the foundation of trust. A player who knows the period will disengage if the collar height is wrong.
- **Documentary lighting authenticity:** Period photography was lit by available light — windows, candles, desk lamps, street torches. No modern electrical feel. The shadows fall the way they fall because the light sources are limited and placed at human height. Apply this to our interior lighting: key light from a window at 45 degrees, diffused by paper screens; candle and lamp sources below eye level in underground spaces.
- **Rationing and scarcity visual language:** 1940s East Asian urban photography documents visible war scarcity — patched clothing, repaired building facades, inconsistent infrastructure, simple food presentation. The occupation is most real in what it removes: the abundance that existed before. Use scarcity as a visual system — enemy and collaborator spaces show least scarcity (they have access); underground spaces show more.
- **Surveillance tension in street photography:** The best period documentary photography from occupied cities captures a specific social tension — people aware of being watched, adjusting their behavior accordingly. This is our default emotional state in public spaces. Apply it to our exterior scenes: every character in enemy territory is performing, even when they think no one is looking.

**What to AVOID:**
- Do not reference propaganda photography (Japanese Imperial Army promotional imagery, CCP revolutionary posters) — these are constructed narratives, not documentary evidence. They skew toward ideology and away from lived experience.
- Do not reference romanticized pre-war nostalgia photography (1930s Shanghai art deco glamour) — this era is after that period. Our period is defined by what was lost, not by what existed before.
- Period photography of active combat zones uses different visual rules than urban occupation. Our setting is urban occupation, not battlefield — do not apply battlefield photography conventions.
- Do not use photographs as direct reference for character poses or compositions. Photograph composition is constrained by camera position; our illustration composition is not.

**Pillar Connection:** Primarily serves **身份即枷锁 (Identity Is a Cage)**. Documentary photography from occupied cities shows how ordinary people adapt their public behavior under surveillance — the performative compliance, the careful not-seeing, the double knowledge. This is the specific psychological texture our visual identity must capture. Period photography proves that this was a real human experience, not a narrative device. It grounds our pillars in documented history.
